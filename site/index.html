Operations & continuous improvement · Brightline Instruments (case study)

Keep parts coming, make the line faster, and check the automation

A quarter of operations work at a small electronics assembler building two handheld devices: choosing suppliers on total cost, warning about shortages before they stop the line, finding the bottleneck with a time study, cutting the top defect, and automating quote entry only as far as it can be trusted.

1 · Purchasing

Which supplier, on what basis?

Lowest price isn't lowest cost. Weight what matters; the pick updates for every part.

Landed cost adds expected defect cost (rework at ~4× part price × the supplier's defect rate) and expected disruption from late deliveries.

2 · Shortage early warning

Projected on-hand, week by week

Stock today, plus open purchase orders when they're now expected, minus what the build plan consumes. Red is a shortage; amber is under one week of cover.

3 · Time study

Find the bottleneck

30 observed cycles per station. Interruptions (part hunting, fixture jams) are logged separately and kept out of the standard time; a 12% allowance is added.

5 · Automation, verified

Reading supplier quotes

An extraction step reads part, supplier, price, minimum order and lead time out of quote emails. Before trusting it, every result was checked against the true values.

What the review check caught

Data is generated for this case study: a fictional assembler, its suppliers, build plan, time studies, defect log and quote emails. Assumptions (allowance, defect cost multiple, disruption cost) are stated where they're used. Code and tests are in the repository.
